# Shaping the studio with Mya

## What we are building for

A calm workspace for Mya and her partner, not a customer-facing showcase.
Success means fewer things to remember, less duplicate work, and more time away
from business admin. Keep the cherry, ivory, and blush palette. Use photos where
they help identify or preview work, not to decorate every screen.

The current home screen offers a few clear actions, a short review summary, and
planned content. "Help me navigate" stays available on every main screen.
Client records and financial tools are not implemented yet.

## A relaxed conversation together (about 30 minutes)

Start with her day, not a feature checklist. Let Mya demonstrate what she already
does on her phone. Do not copy real client or financial details into the prototype.

### 1. Understand the everyday work

- Walk through a recent workday, from first message to finishing the last client.
- What does she repeatedly look up, write down, forget, or enter twice?
- Which task follows her home when she would rather be with her kids?
- What already works well and should stay as it is?
- Which booking, payment, calendar, and messaging tools does she use?

### 2. Try the prototype without coaching

Ask her to find a draft, add a photo, and plan a post. Watch where she pauses.
Then ask her to find the help button. Make it clear that confusing parts are the
tool's responsibility, not hers.

- Which words or buttons feel unclear?
- Is the text comfortable? Is there too much on screen?
- Would she prefer a different language?
- Does she want reminders, or would they feel like more pressure?
- What should she see first when she opens the studio?

### 3. Decide what each of you handles

- Who drafts, reviews, and publishes content?
- What needs Mya's approval, and what can her partner handle?
- Who would enter client history and money records?
- Which information should each person be able to see or change?

### 4. Explore optional business tools

Only keep ideas that solve an actual problem:

- Client book: the minimum useful contact details, service preferences, history,
  photo permissions, and follow-ups.
- Business overview: recorded service income, tips, expenses, and monthly totals.
  Distinguish take-home estimates from accounting profit.
- Existing services: prefer integration or import over duplicate booking/payment
  systems. Do not store payment-card details.

## Leave the meeting with five decisions

1. The three tasks that would save her the most effort.
2. The single most useful home-screen action.
3. What she and her partner each own.
4. What to leave out for now.
5. What would make a one-week trial feel successful.

Capture each priority with this small template:

## Restructure in small, agreed steps

1. **Simplify with her:** revise labels, navigation, home priorities, and text
   size based on the trial. Keep optional detail collapsed; avoid more dashboards.
2. **Create the secure foundation:** individual sign-ins, shared database,
   access controls, protected media, backup/restore, export and deletion.
   The current hosted prototype is not private just because it is for internal use.
3. **Add the first useful business task:** client book or money overview, according
   to her priorities. Keep it separate from the content workflow and easy to hide.
4. **Try it for a week:** check whether it reduces time and mental load. Change or
   remove what does not help before adding the next feature.

Do not promise revenue outcomes. Measure practical relief: less repeated entry,
fewer forgotten follow-ups, tasks completed without help, and time saved.

## Separate future customer experience

If Mya later wants a public page, design it separately for services, portfolio,
and booking links. It must not expose the workspace, client book, financials,
internal notes, or private media. That is a later decision, not part of this phase.
